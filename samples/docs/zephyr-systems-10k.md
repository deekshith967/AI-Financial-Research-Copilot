# Zephyr Systems Inc. — FY2025 Form 10-K (Fictional Sample)

This document is a fictional sample created for local testing of a retrieval
pipeline. Zephyr Systems Inc. ("Zephyr", ticker ZPHY) is not a real company,
and all figures are invented.

## Item 1. Business

Zephyr Systems Inc. operates the Stratoserve platform, a fleet of
helium-cooled data centers designed for high-density AI training workloads.
The Stratoserve platform uses a closed-loop helium circulation system that
lowers cooling energy consumption by roughly 37 percent compared with
conventional air-cooled facilities.

The company sells capacity through multi-year reserved contracts and
on-demand burst usage. As of fiscal year-end 2025, Zephyr operated nine
Stratoserve sites across Texas, Oregon, and Finland, with two additional
sites under construction in Iceland.

Reserved contracts contributed 78 percent of FY2025 revenue, with a weighted
average remaining term of 4.2 years. On-demand burst usage grew 41 percent
year over year as smaller AI labs adopted the platform for short training
runs. The company also launched Coldpath, a thermal scheduling software
module that improves cluster utilisation by shifting workloads between sites
based on real-time cooling headroom and electricity prices.

Competition comes from large cloud providers operating their own immersion
cooling facilities and from colocation operators retrofitting liquid cooling.
Zephyr believes its helium closed-loop design is differentiated because it
requires no water usage and operates efficiently in cold and warm climates.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors

Helium supply risk: Zephyr depends on a stable supply of industrial helium.
A prolonged disruption in global helium production, including outages at
major liquefaction plants, could force the company to throttle cooling
capacity and delay new site launches.

Energy cost risk: Electricity represented 44 percent of site operating cost
in FY2025. Sustained increases in grid power prices in Texas or Oregon would
compress gross margins if reserved contract pricing cannot be adjusted.

Customer concentration: Northwind Retail Group accounted for 31 percent of
FY2025 revenue. Loss or downsizing of this anchor customer would materially
affect utilisation rates at three sites.

Construction risk: The Iceland build-out faces permitting and geothermal
interconnection reviews that could delay commissioning beyond fiscal 2027.

Technology risk: If immersion or direct-to-chip liquid cooling becomes the
industry standard for AI clusters, demand for helium-based systems could
decline, and the company would need to re-engineer the Stratoserve platform
at significant cost.

## Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis

Revenue for fiscal year 2025 was 412 million dollars, an increase of 18
percent over fiscal 2024, driven by expansion of Stratoserve reserved
contracts. Gross margin was 61 percent, up from 57 percent, primarily due to
lower cooling energy intensity at the Finland sites.

Research and development expense was 96 million dollars, concentrated on the
next-generation Stratoserve-X cooling loop and the Coldpath thermal
scheduling software.

Operating cash flow was 154 million dollars. Capital expenditures were 210
million dollars, mostly related to the Iceland construction program. The
company ended the year with 305 million dollars in cash and equivalents and
a 150 million dollar undrawn revolving credit facility.

Looking ahead to fiscal 2026, management expects revenue of 470 to 485
million dollars, with the commissioning of the first Iceland site as the
primary swing factor. Management also expects helium procurement costs to
remain elevated and has budgeted a mid-single-digit increase in site
operating costs as a result.
